After my last dive I realised the sonar didn't do what I wanted. It maps a strip under the ship as you move, like a lawnmower. That's how real survey ships work, but the holotable is for planning: you gather round it and decide whether to go deeper, into the canyon, or back. For that you need to know what's around you right now.

## One loud pulse

So now there's an active ping. Press F, and the ship sends one pulse in every direction. You see a ring of light sweep out across the seabed, climbing over hills, with faint height lines glowing behind it before they fade. You hear it too: a deep, long tone that rings through the whole hull, with echoes coming back.

![A ring of sonar light sweeping over the hills, and the ship's floodlight on a nearer hill.](/Thalassa/images/devlog/0023-ping.jpg)

As the wave spreads, the ground it reaches gets charted, out to 1.2 km. It's coarser than the survey strip, 100-metre cells instead of 25, but it's all around you. And it can't see through hills: ground hidden behind a ridge stays dark. If you want to see more, go higher. I like that a lot.

The holotable fills in with it, and now shows the 1.2 km around the ship:

![The holotable on the bridge, glowing with the terrain around the ship after a ping.](/Thalassa/images/devlog/0023-holotable.jpg)

## Upgrades

Not every ship will have this. What your sonar can do will depend on how you've upgraded it: no ping at all, a short one, or the long one. For testing, the ship has the best, and G switches between grades. Later, pinging will be loud in another sense too. Things out there may hear it.

## A chart without edges

This also fixed a problem from before: the chart only covered the six kilometres around the start. Now it covers the whole ocean, wherever you take the ship. Under the hood, the chart is shared between players by sending only what changed, so it can grow without choking the network. In tests with pings every ten seconds, the host's and the client's charts stayed in step, even with a quarter-second of lag and dropped packets.

## What this does not prove

I've only seen the ring in screenshots, not felt a dive with it yet. The ping charts ground but not rocks or creatures. A player joining late still receives the whole chart at once, which will need work after hours of exploring. And there's no wave in the water itself yet, only on the ground.
